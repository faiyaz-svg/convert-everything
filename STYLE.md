# Writing style

Rules for every word a user sees: labels, buttons, help text, notes, error messages, the README and release notes.

## Basics

- Write for someone who just wants their file converted. Say what happens, in plain words.
- Short sentences, but not all the same length.
- Buttons say what they do: "Convert 3 files", "Show in folder", "Merge 4 files". Not "Submit" or "OK".
- Error messages say what went wrong and what to do: "This archive is protected with a password. Enter it under More options."
- Headings and labels are in sentence case: "Where converted files go", not "Where Converted Files Go".
- Name formats the way people know them: "Word (DOCX)", "JPG", "MP4".

## Patterns to avoid

These are signs of AI-generated text, collected from Wikipedia's "Signs of AI writing" page and from studies of words that language models overuse. One or two can appear by chance in human writing; the problem is when they pile up. Treat them as hard rules here.

### Words: never use

delve, tapestry, testament, pivotal, underscore (as a verb), intricate, meticulous, showcase, garner, bolster, interplay, vibrant, multifaceted, realm, groundbreaking, renowned, nestled, seamless, seamlessly, commendable, noteworthy, invaluable, transformative, unparalleled, unveil, harness, elucidate, burgeoning, foster, leverage (as a verb), boasts, deep dive, "key role", "key factor", unlock, elevate, supercharge, empower, effortless, blazing, robust.

### Words: use rarely

crucial, vital, essential, comprehensive, notably, additionally, moreover, furthermore, ultimately, enhance, powerful, valuable, significant. Prefer a plain word: "needed", "also", "so", "big".

### Phrases and patterns: never use

- "serves as", "stands as" where "is" works.
- Inflated importance: "plays a crucial role", "is a cornerstone of".
- A trailing "-ing" clause that adds vague commentary: "..., making it easy to share".
- Disclaimers: "It's important to note that", "It's worth noting". State the point directly.
- Chatbot lines: "Great question", "Certainly!", "Let's dive in", "I hope this helps".
- Negative parallelism: "not only X but also Y", "it's not X, it's Y".
- Groups of three used for rhythm ("fast, simple, and private"). A real list of three things is fine.
- Exclamation marks.

### Punctuation and characters

- No em dashes. Use a comma, a colon, parentheses or a new sentence.
- En dashes only in number ranges, or write "10 to 12".
- Straight quotes and apostrophes only.
- No emoji, no unicode bullets.

### Don't over-correct

Wikipedia also flags the opposite extreme: text that never uses a colon or semicolon and keeps every sentence the same length. Write like a person explaining something across a desk.
